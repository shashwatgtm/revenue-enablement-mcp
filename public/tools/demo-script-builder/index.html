Demo Script
Part 1: Opening (4 minutes, from 0:00)

Say: "Thanks for making the time. Over the next 30 minutes I will take you through route planning software for last-mile delivery, with the Head of Last-Mile Operations in the room, with Third-party logistics in mind."

Say: "For context on who is talking: Re-plan a day's routes when 20 orders change; One dispatch view across all 12 cities." (a claim you gave; have the source ready)

Say: "What I would like by the end of this session is a clear next step that we both agree."

Say: "The plan: first I confirm that I have your problem right, then 16 minutes of demo in 3 steps (choose one buyer task, confirm a safe demonstration and agree the evidence and decision), then your questions, then the next step."

Ask: "Before I share my screen: Head of Last-Mile Operations, what would make these 30 minutes worth it for you?"

Part 2: Confirm (4 minutes, from 4:00)

Say: "I have not been told your main problem, so I will confirm it before showing a capability."

Ask the Head of Last-Mile Operations: "Before I show confirm a safe demonstration: which part of the day goes wrong most often, and what does it cost?"

Ask: "What is the one thing you most want solved?"

Part 3: Demo (16 minutes, from 8:00)

Say: "We have not agreed what to demonstrate yet. Let us choose the task and test before opening a live system."

Step 1: Choose one buyer task (about 5 minutes)
Ask: "Which task should we test, using one recent example you choose, and what result would count as a pass?"
Check: Record the task, chosen example and expected observation before selecting a feature.

Step 2: Confirm a safe demonstration (about 5 minutes)
Ask: "Which confirmed capability can run that task in an isolated test, who will operate it, and which approvals are required?"
Check: Record the confirmed capability, operator, test environment and required approvals before a demonstration.

Step 3: Agree the evidence and decision (about 5 minutes)
Ask: "Who will observe the test and approve the result, where will we record failures, and what evidence would justify the next step?"
Check: Record the approver, pass condition and evidence location before agreeing the next step.

Part 4: Discussion (4 minutes, from 24:00)

Say: "Which condition is still unresolved before we prepare the test?"

Buyer may ask: "We already have a TMS"
Say: "Let me lay route planning software for last-mile delivery's parts next to what you run today, and mark what is duplicated, what only one of them does and what you could retire. Where a tool you already have does a job well, I will say keep it. Sentence read: We already have a TMS."
Ask: "Which tools do you run today, and which of them do you trust least?"
Playbook for logistics tech, last mile delivery, not for saying aloud: Ask which daily decisions it does not make today (re planning when orders change, address cleaning, driver allocation) and position the product as working with it, not replacing it.

Buyer may ask: "Drivers will not use a new app"
Say: "Let us plan adoption with the people who will use route planning software for last-mile delivery every day: a small first group, one measure of use agreed before we begin, and a named owner on your side. Their results make the case for everyone else. Sentence read: Drivers will not use a new app."
Ask: "Who would use it every day, and what would make them keep using it?"
Playbook for logistics tech, last mile delivery, not for saying aloud: Offer a pilot at one hub with an offline first driver app and agree how adoption is measured before the pilot starts.

Buyer may ask: "Integration effort"
Say: "Let us take it system by system: for each one I will say whether the link is built in, goes through an API or needs a file transfer, who builds it and who owns it on your side. I would like your IT owner on a technical call to agree which data moves in which direction. Sentence read: Integration effort."
Ask: "Which system is the master record for this data today, and who owns the connection?"
Playbook for logistics tech, last mile delivery, not for saying aloud: Name the systems involved (transport, warehouse, ERP, order management) and the few data fields needed, and agree who on the buyer side owns each.

Part 5: Close (2 minutes, from 28:00)

Say: "Before we agree a pilot, let us review each recorded acceptance condition. Who approves it, what observation counts as a pass, and which conditions remain unagreed? I will send those conditions and the evidence for your confirmation."

Say: "Here is what we covered. We discussed the task, safe test and approval evidence. No capability has been demonstrated in this planning conversation."

Say: "What would be the right next step from here, and who should be part of it?"
The next step to offer, from this sector: agree the pilot hub, the baseline and the measure (cost per delivery, first-attempt delivery, dispatch planning time).

If yes. Say: "I will send a summary of what we covered and an invite for the next step. Who else should be on it?"

If hesitant. Say: "Which of the problems we played back is still not answered for you? I want you to have everything you need."

After The Demo
  • Send the written answers to: We already have a TMS; Drivers will not use a new app; Integration effort.
  • Send the summary promised in the close, and brief your champion separately.
Check before you say it
  • "We already have a TMS": what each part of route planning software for last-mile delivery covers that the current tools do not, and what the current tools cover that route planning software for last-mile delivery does not.
  • "Drivers will not use a new app": the training and support route planning software for last-mile delivery gives a first group, and the adoption you have seen with similar customers (only with their consent).
  • "Integration effort": which of the buyer's systems route planning software for last-mile delivery connects to today, in what way and with what limits, from your integration documentation.
Sector notes: logistics tech, last mile delivery
  • Objections this sector often raises: we already have a tms; drivers will not use a new app; integration effort; our margins are too thin for this.
  • A proof point that lands: A before and after of cost per delivery or first attempt delivery at one hub, measured over a full cycle of busy and quiet weeks.
  • What a logistics tech, last mile delivery buyer wants to see: show only what route planning software for last-mile delivery really does:
  • A live re-plan when an order changes after the vehicles have left
  • The dispatcher view and the driver view of the same day
  • A before-and-after of cost per delivery or first-attempt delivery for one hub, if you have one you may show
  • Words this sector's buyers use: dispatch, fleet, last mile, route plan, first attempt delivery, proof of delivery, third party logistics provider, cost per delivery, delivery SLA, delivery window. Use them where they are true for the prospect.
Reading the answer

The script supports an acceptance discussion for route planning software for last-mile delivery. Start with one buyer chosen example: agree the expected result, demonstrate only a confirmed capability, then ask the buyer to accept or reject what happened. Check whether the buyer chosen record reaches the agreed result; if this cannot be shown, record a gap rather than implying the capability exists. The conclusion needs review if the buyer rejects the observed result, the required capability is unavailable, or the objection remains unresolved.

Decisions by role

Proposed owners. Confirm the named person before acting.

Founder: ask the buyer whether the test addresses "the unresolved buyer objection" before endorsing the demonstration; record the observation that would disprove fit.

CEO: have the delivery lead confirm a safe test environment and a named operator for route planning software for last-mile delivery before committing staff; hold any live change that has no approval.

CMO: compare the claims in the route planning software for last-mile delivery demo with the recorded screen or service evidence before reusing them; do not infer a performance improvement from a walkthrough.

CRO: agree with the buyer how to check whether the buyer chosen record reaches the agreed result before the demonstration; record pass, failure or not demonstrated against the same condition.

Demand generation head: use the unresolved objection "the unresolved buyer objection" to review the next invitation list; check that invitees face this problem before scaling demo outreach.

To sharpen this, give: attendees (it would add a row and a question for each person, and choose the parts they should see); key pain points (it would choose which parts are shown and what the playback says; now the steps follow the order of your description); competitor context (it would add a side by side line and sharpen the difference answers); known objections (it would replace the usual objections of the sector with the ones you expect); desired outcome (it would change the close; now it agrees the next step); your solution (list the parts of the product with a few words on what each does; it would make the steps the product's own parts).

Suggested timings, lengths and counts: adjust them to your own.

Region context: Which region applies: India, Southeast Asia, Middle East, UK, Western Europe or North America? Add it to your description and rerun.

How to read the answer

The builder creates a timed, five-part demo plan (Opening, Confirm, Demo in 3 steps, Discussion, and Close) scaled to your Demo duration (such as 30 minutes), along with a Who sees what table and spoken Say: and Ask: prompts. At the top, Acceptance conditions to agree before the script warns you that if no Must show features or Key pain points were supplied, the three demo steps use generic seller workflows (The workflow the buyer described, The controls, reports and audit trail, and Where it sits among the systems) and you must confirm the buyer's main problem in Part 2: Confirm before sharing your screen.

What to do next

  • Founder: Before running the demo, fill in Key pain points, Must show features, and the specific parts of Your solution so the three demo steps show your actual product flows rather than generic placeholders.
  • CEO: Make sure every demo ends with Part 5: Close by agreeing on the exact capabilities, pass conditions, and participants required for a pilot.
  • CMO: Supply the industry example data and competitive side-by-side talking points so the solutions engineer can run a real case from the buyer's sector in Step 1.
  • CRO: Train reps to follow Part 2: Confirm strictly: never share the screen until the primary audience member confirms the one problem they most want solved in these 30 minutes.
  • Demand generation head: Use the After The Demo follow-up step to send prospects a recap template and a link to relevant technical or security documentation.

Why come back

Re-run the builder for each live deal once discovery is complete, adding the full list of Attendees, Key pain points, Must show features, and Known objections, or when switching from a First look to a Technical deep dive, Executive overview, or Proof of concept. The tool applies the same fixed rules every time. It does not call an AI model, and this site does not keep what you enter.
