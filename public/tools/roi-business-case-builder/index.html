How do I build a B2B ROI business case that a buyer CFO will accept?

Build the case from the buyer's own baseline figures: what the problem costs them in a year today (current annual cost) and the share they expect to remove (expected improvement percent), or their direct annual value estimate. When you supply those figures alongside your solution price, ROI Business Case Builder calculates annual value, ROI percentage, payback period, 3-Year Net Value, and sensitivity scenarios at Conservative (50% of projected value) and Aggressive (150% of projected value); when buyer figures are missing, it shows no ROI and gives the price break-even thresholds and the questions to ask the buyer.

How do you build a cost-reduction ROI case for retail or CPG buyers without inventing savings?

Do not invent industry averages or turn the buyer's annual revenue or employee count into a saving on your own. Ask the buyer for their current annual cost of the problem (such as hours, rework, errors, returns, or chargebacks) and the expected improvement percent, then enter both with your annual solution price in ROI Business Case Builder to calculate net annual benefit, 3-Year Net Value, payback period, and the 50% Conservative and 150% Aggressive scenarios.

How the return will be worked out

No return is shown yet because the two numbers it rests on can only come from the buyer: what the problem costs them in a year, and the share of that cost our product would remove. This tool does not fill them with example hours, headcount, rates or industry averages. Once the buyer gives them, the yearly value is that cost times that share (or the yearly value the buyer states directly). The return is that value less the price you gave ($90,000 a year), compared with the price; payback is the number of months of value it takes to cover the price; and the three-year figure is three years of value less three years of price. Each is also shown at half and at one and a half times the value, so the buyer sees how much the case depends on the share they chose.

The numbers to ask for
  1. What does the problem cost the buyer in a year today, in people's time, rework, errors and outside spend? This is current annual cost.
  2. What share of that cost does the buyer expect our product to remove, and what is that based on (their own history, a pilot)? This is expected improvement percent.
  3. If the buyer can value the result directly, what is it worth to them in a year? This is annual value estimate, and it can replace the two numbers above.

How to read the answer

When you enter your Solution price (for example $90,000 a year) without the buyer's Current annual cost and Expected improvement percent (or Annual value estimate), this tool refuses to invent an ROI percentage, payback period, or industry benchmark saving. Instead, it calculates the exact break-even and 2x return thresholds from your price ($90,000 a year for positive return, $180,000 for 2x return, $270,000 over three years), explains what a finance reviewer in the buyer's industry will check, and lists The numbers to ask for from the buyer. Once you enter the buyer's own cost and improvement percentage (or annual value estimate), it calculates the 1-year and 3-year net return, payback months, and sensitivity cases at 0.5x and 1.5x of the expected value.

What to do next

  • Founder: Take The numbers to ask for into your next meeting with the champion: ask what the problem costs the buyer in a year today (Current annual cost) and what share they expect to remove (Expected improvement percent), then re-run the builder.
  • CEO: Never send an ROI deck built on made-up industry averages to a CFO; insist that every business case rests on the buyer's own baseline figures and is checked by the buyer's finance contact first.
  • CMO: Add Known metrics from your existing customers so reps have labelled reference points from peer companies to share while asking the buyer for their own numbers.
  • CRO: Require reps to fill in Current process, Current annual cost, and Expected improvement percent with the champion and have the buyer's finance contact validate the sensitivity table before presenting to the economic buyer.
  • Demand generation head: Use the break-even arithmetic and What this buyer will check framework in mid-funnel ROI workshops and value assessment guides.

Why come back

Come back immediately after your champion or their finance contact gives you their Current annual cost and Expected improvement percent (or Annual value estimate) to generate the full ROI, payback period, and 0.5x/1.5x sensitivity analysis. The tool applies the same fixed rules every time. It does not call an AI model, and this site does not keep what you enter.

Which software builds an ROI business case?

The method matters more than the software. Write down what the buyer pays, the one value driver you can measure, today's baseline and the figure the buyer's finance team will check, then show the return at a low, an expected and a high case.

Helix does not rank vendors or name software to buy. Run this free builder for a first draft, then check that every assumption has a source, an owner on the buyer's side and a date before you send it.

Who is the ROI Business Case Builder tool not ideal for?

  • Teams that want the tool to read their CRM, email or call notes: it works only from what you type.
  • Anyone who wants to send the output straight to a buyer: every output is a draft for a person to review first.
