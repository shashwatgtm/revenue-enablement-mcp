Rule #2: Understand the Real Ask

Discovery Questions:

  • "Help me understand what's driving the discount request."
  • "Is this a budget issue or a value perception issue?"
  • "What would need to happen for full price to work?"
  • "Who's asking for the discount? What's their concern?"
Rule #3: Reframe Value, Not Price

Value Reframe:

"Before we discuss price, let's revisit what this has already delivered: Failed first deliveries down from 1 in 6 to 1 in 10 in the pilot city.
At $30,000, is that result worth more to you than the 20% you are asking for?"

Tactical Responses
"We need a better price"

Don't Say: "Let me see what I can do."

Do Say:

  • "Help me understand what 'better' means. Is this about budget or value?"
  • "What specifically is the concern with the price?"
  • "What would need to happen for our current pricing to work?"
"The price direction is not clear; compare the written quotes"

Don't Say: "We can match that."

Do Say:

  • "That's interesting. What's included in their price?"
  • "Let's compare apples to apples. What capabilities are you comparing?"
  • "Price is one factor. What are the other criteria that matter?"
"We can only pay a lower figure"

Don't Say: "I'll talk to my manager."

Do Say:

  • "I understand budget constraints. Help me understand how that number was determined."
  • "Let's look at scope. What would need to change to fit that budget?"
  • "What if we structured payments differently? For example pay in two instalments of $15,000 instead of $30,000 at once (your deal value split in two)."
Discount Approval Framework
When to Consider Discounting:
  • [ ] This is a strategic account (logo value)
  • [ ] There's genuine budget constraint (verified)
  • [ ] We're getting something valuable in return
  • [ ] Competitive pressure is real and verified
  • [ ] This won't set bad precedent
When to Hold the Line:
  • [ ] Value case is strong
  • [ ] No competitive pressure
  • [ ] Buyer is bluffing
  • [ ] Would set bad precedent
  • [ ] Deal is already won
Escalation Path

If discount approval is needed:

  1. Document the ask: Why, how much, what we get
  2. Show your work: What you've tried
  3. Make a recommendation: Not just the percentage the buyer asked for
  4. Get approval before offering: Never surprise leadership
Sector notes: logistics tech
  • Objections this sector often raises: we already have a system for this; integration effort; our operations team will not change how they work; the cost is hard to justify on our margins.
  • A proof point that lands: A before and after of on time delivery or cost per shipment on one lane, site or region, measured over a full cycle of busy and quiet weeks.
  • Words this sector's buyers use: shipment, carrier, consignment, lead time, exception, service level, proof of delivery, third party logistics provider, transport management system, warehouse management system. Use them where they are true for the prospect.
Reading the answer

This is preparation for negotiating the supplied offer. The supplied deal amount 30,000 and alternative quote "the unverified alternative quote" are not a like for like comparison until scope, period and exclusions match. Suggested terms require approval. The conclusion needs review if matched quote scope or period changes the comparison, delivery cost removes the margin, or the buyer cannot substantiate the claimed value.

Decisions by role

Proposed owners. Confirm the named person before acting.

Founder: ask the buyer which requirement makes "Failed first deliveries down from 1 in 6 to 1 in 10 in the pilot city" valuable before defending the 30,000 amount; retain any unmeasured benefit as a hypothesis.

CEO: have finance reconcile the 30,000 amount with its period, delivery cost and minimum approved margin before authorizing a concession; name the person who can approve it.

CMO: verify the source and scope of "Failed first deliveries down from 1 in 6 to 1 in 10 in the pilot city" before using it to justify price; remove unsupported savings or response time claims.

CRO: obtain the scope, term and exclusions behind "the unverified alternative quote" and the 30,000 offer before discussing a discount; record unresolved differences.

Demand generation head: check that any the supplied offer pricing message uses the approved scope and period before reuse; do not turn this negotiated quote into a public price promise.

How to read the answer

The guide calculates Revenue at Risk and Post-Discount Value from your Deal value and Discount requested (for example, a 20% discount on $30,000 puts $6,000 at risk for a $24,000 post-discount value), then walks through a Leverage Assessment (Your Leverage vs Their Leverage), a 3-rule Negotiation Strategy (Never Discount Without Getting Something, Understand the Real Ask, and Reframe Value, Not Price), Tactical Responses (Don't Say vs Do Say), a Discount Approval Framework, and an Escalation Path. If you do not enter Your leverage or Buyer leverage, it prints checklists of common leverage points for you to tick.

What to do next

  • Founder: Enforce Rule #1: Never Discount Without Getting Something by requiring a trade from the Acceptable Trades table (a longer term, upfront payment, wider scope, or written case study and reference rights) before any price concession is made.
  • CEO: Set clear sign-off thresholds using the Discount Approval Framework and Escalation Path so reps never offer an unapproved discount or set a bad precedent.
  • CMO: Supply the quantified customer value points under Value delivered so reps can reframe price against the cost of the buyer's problem under Rule #3.
  • CRO: Coach reps on the Tactical Responses (Do Say vs Don't Say) before their negotiation call, including offering split payment instalments ($15,000 twice instead of $30,000 at once) when the objection is cash flow rather than value.
  • Demand generation head: Reinforce value perception during late-stage negotiations by sharing peer customer proof and executive briefs with the buying committee.

Why come back

Run the guide again whenever a deal enters a different negotiation scenario (Budget objection, Competitor pricing, Procurement pressure, Multi-year negotiation, Enterprise agreement, or Renewal negotiation) or once you know the competitor's written quote and your specific leverage points. The tool applies the same fixed rules every time. It does not call an AI model, and this site does not keep what you enter.
