Strategic Analysis
Power Map (To Be Mapped)

You gave no contacts. Start with these questions (written for logistics tech):

  1. Who owns the problem Lanehop solves? In logistics tech this is usually: the Head of Logistics or Last-Mile Operations champions
  2. Who controls the budget? The COO or Head of Supply Chain signs.
  3. Who will use the solution daily?
  4. Who must approve the purchase, and what do they check?

Roles to look for: Chief Operating Officer, Head of Supply Chain, Head of Last-Mile Operations, Fleet Manager, Transport Manager, Head of IT.

Current Footprint

Lanehop route planning in 3 of their 12 cities

Whitespace Opportunities

Confirm what exactly the account uses of Lanehop today and what it still does by other means.

  1. Adjacent use cases: which other teams or units feel the same problem (ask your contacts above who else is affected)
  2. Deeper use: where the teams you already serve still work around Lanehop
  3. Cross-sell: complementary parts of your offer they do not have
  4. Proof first: show the result in the footprint you have before asking for more; in logistics tech buyers trust this form of proof: a before and after of cost per delivery or first-attempt delivery at one hub, measured over a full cycle of busy and quiet weeks
Competitive Intelligence Needed

No threats were given. Ask:

  1. "Who else is solving this problem for you today, including doing it by hand?"
  2. "What would need to change for you to consider alternatives?"
  3. "What has worked and not worked with the current way?"
90-Day Account Plan
Days 1-30: Deepen the relationship (by 2026-11-02)

Objectives:

  • [ ] Complete stakeholder mapping (all decision makers identified)
  • [ ] Understand current state and pain points (in logistics tech, ask about cost per delivery, first-attempt delivery rate, on-time delivery)
  • [ ] Identify 2 or 3 potential champions
  • [ ] Document the competitive landscape, including doing it by hand

Key Activities:

  1. Schedule discovery meetings with the people who own the problem
  2. Map the org chart and reporting lines against the usual committee in logistics tech
  3. Identify the business trigger behind this account's priorities

Success Criteria:

  • A champion identified and engaged
  • Pain points documented and quantified in their numbers
  • Initial business case hypothesis
Days 31-60: Engagement (by 2026-12-02)

Objectives:

  • [ ] Demonstrate value to key stakeholders
  • [ ] Build the business case with the account's own figures (the ROI Business Case Builder tool needs their cost or value figures)
  • [ ] Multi-thread across the buying committee, including Chief Operating Officer and Head of Supply Chain
  • [ ] Address technical and procurement requirements

Key Activities:

  1. Run a demo or working session on the account's own case
  2. Develop the ROI model with the account's data
  3. Connect the champion with reference customers, if you have them
  4. Agree a proof: a before and after of cost per delivery or first-attempt delivery at one hub, measured over a full cycle of busy and quiet weeks

Success Criteria:

  • Business case accepted by the economic buyer
  • Technical requirements validated
  • Procurement process understood
Days 61-90: Close/Expand (by 2027-01-01)

Objectives:

  • [ ] Close expansion deal
  • [ ] Establish success metrics and implementation plan
  • [ ] Set foundation for future expansion
  • [ ] Document wins and learnings

Key Activities:

  1. Finalize commercial terms
  2. Complete procurement process
  3. Kick off implementation planning
  4. Identify the next expansion opportunity

Success Criteria:

  • Contract signed
  • Implementation scheduled
  • Expansion opportunities documented
Account Intelligence
What to Research
  • The words this sector's buyers use: dispatch, fleet, last-mile, route plan, first-attempt delivery, proof of delivery, 3PL, cost per delivery, delivery SLA, TMS; listen for them in what the account publishes
  • Recent news and press releases that touch cost per delivery or first-attempt delivery rate
  • Earnings calls and investor presentations (if the company is public)
  • Hiring and org changes for the roles above
  • Job postings for priorities
Key Questions to Answer
  1. What are their top 3 strategic priorities this year?
  2. How do they measure success (is it cost per delivery or first-attempt delivery rate?)?
  3. What's their budget cycle?
  4. Who has buying authority?
  5. What would prevent them from buying?

Who is Account Plan Builder not ideal for?

  • Anyone who cannot give the inputs listed above; the answer is built only from them.
  • Teams that want the tools to read their CRM, email or call notes: they work only from what you type.
  • Sending anything straight to a buyer: every output is a draft for a person to review first.

How do I use Account Plan Builder from Claude?

How does Account Plan Builder work?

No sign-in. A web browser is enough, and the answer comes back in a second or two.

  1. Fill in the form above, or press Fill in an example.
  2. Press Run Account Plan Builder.
  3. Read the result on this page, then copy it and edit it before you use it.

It applies fixed rules in the Revenue Enablement code to the inputs above and returns text. It does not call an AI model, does not look anything up, and does not store what you type. The web form and the AI assistant connector run the same tool code, so the same input gives the same answer in both.
