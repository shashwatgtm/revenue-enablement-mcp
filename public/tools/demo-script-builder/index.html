Pre-Demo Preparation
Who you are showing it to

Head of Last-Mile Operations is an operations leader. They care about daily execution, service levels and cost per unit of work, and worry about disruption to live operations and a team that will not use the new way. They need to see a pilot in one place with a before-and-after measure.

Research Checklist
  • Review previous conversations and notes
  • Research company news and priorities
  • Understand attendee roles and concerns
  • Prepare relevant customer examples: a before and after of cost per delivery or first-attempt delivery at one hub, measured over a full cycle of busy and quiet weeks
  • Use the words this sector's buyers use, where they are true for the prospect: dispatch, fleet, last-mile, route plan, first-attempt delivery, proof of delivery, 3PL, cost per delivery, delivery SLA, TMS
  • Test the demo environment
Technical Setup
  • Demo environment ready
  • Sample data loaded
  • Screen sharing tested
  • Backup plan ready
What a logistics tech buyer wants to see

Show only what Lanehop really does:

  • A live re-plan when an order changes after the vehicles have left
  • The dispatcher view and the driver view of the same day
  • A before-and-after of cost per delivery or first-attempt delivery for one hub, if you have one you may show
Demo Script
Part 1: Opening (4 minutes)

0:00 Introduction

"Thanks everyone for joining. I will walk you through Lanehop today.

Before I share my screen, I want to make sure we cover what's most important to you. Head of Last-Mile Operations: what would make this 30 minutes valuable for you?"

Wait for the response: it shapes your demo.

1:00 Agenda Setting

"Here's my plan for today:

  1. A quick check of what I've learned about your situation
  2. How Lanehop addresses those specific needs
  3. Time for questions and discussion
  4. Agreeing next steps

Does that work for everyone?"

Part 2: Discovery Confirmation (4 minutes)

4:00 Validate Understanding

"Before I show you anything, let me confirm what I've learned so the demo is relevant:

I have not been told your main pain points, so I will ask: what is the main problem you want solved?

Did I get that right? Anything to add?"

Listen, and adjust the demo to what you hear.

Discovery Questions to Ask:

  • "What's driving your interest in looking at solutions like this?"
  • "What does success look like for you?"
  • "Who else needs to be involved in this decision?"
  • "Which part of the day goes wrong most often, and what does it cost?"
  • "How do you measure it today, and who owns that number?"
Part 3: Solution Demo (16 minutes)

8:00 Transition to Demo

"Great, that confirms what I thought. Let me show you how Lanehop handles those challenges. I'm going to share my screen..."

Share your screen with the demo environment.

Part 4: Discussion (4 minutes)

24:00 Open for Questions

"Let me stop sharing for a moment. What questions do you have about what you've seen?"

Questions to Prepare For in logistics tech:

"We already have a TMS"

Pattern of an answer: Ask which daily decisions the TMS does not make today (re-planning when orders change, address cleaning, driver allocation) and position the product as working with the TMS, not replacing it.

"Drivers will not use a new app"

Pattern of an answer: Offer a pilot at one hub with an offline-first driver app and agree how adoption is measured before the pilot starts.

"Integration effort"

Pattern of an answer: Name the systems involved (TMS, WMS, ERP, order management) and the few data fields needed, and agree who on the buyer side owns each.

"Our margins are too thin for this"

Pattern of an answer: Tie the price to cost per delivery and failed deliveries measured in the buyer's own data, not to a general claim.

"How long does implementation take?"

Give a dated plan, not a promise: the steps from signing to first use, who does what on each side, and what the buyer must have ready (access, data, people). Offer to agree the plan before the contract is signed.

Confirm first: the set-up time you have actually achieved for customers of a similar size, and what Lanehop needs from the buyer; give a range only if you can show it.

"What does it need from our IT team?"

Answer the exact question asked ("What does it need from our IT team?") in two parts: the facts the buyer needs, then what you will do next. If you do not have a fact yet, say when you will have it rather than guess.

Confirm first: the facts behind that question from Lanehop's own documentation.

"What does pricing look like?"

Do not defend the price in isolation. Set out the total cost to the buyer (price, set-up, integration, the team's time, and what the current way of working costs them) and let them compare it with the alternative on the same basis.

Confirm first: Lanehop's price and exactly what it includes; use an alternative's price only from a quote the buyer shows you, not from memory.

Part 5: Close (2 minutes)

28:00 Summarize & Close

"Before we wrap up, let me summarize what we covered:

  1. Your priority → Lanehop: a live re-plan when an order changes after the vehicles have left
  2. Your priority → Lanehop: the dispatcher view and the driver view of the same day
  3. Your priority → Lanehop: a before-and-after of cost per delivery or first-attempt delivery for one hub

The Ask:

"Based on what you've seen, what would be helpful as a next step?

Options might be:

  • Technical deep dive with your team
  • Business case review
  • Reference call with a similar customer (only if one has agreed)
  • A pilot: agree the pilot hub, the baseline and the measure (cost per delivery, first-attempt delivery, dispatch planning time)

What makes sense for you?"

If Positive:

"Great! I'll send a follow-up with the materials we discussed and a calendar invite for the next step. Who else should I include?"

If Hesitant:

"What concerns do you still have? I want to make sure you have everything you need."

Post-Demo Checklist
  • [ ] Send follow-up email within 2 hours
  • [ ] Include demo recording (if recorded)
  • [ ] Send resources promised
  • [ ] Update CRM with notes
  • [ ] Schedule next meeting
  • [ ] Brief champion separately
Demo Tips

Do:

  • Lead with outcomes, not features
  • Ask questions throughout
  • Personalize examples
  • Confirm understanding frequently
  • Leave time for questions

Don't:

  • Show everything you can do
  • Talk more than listen
  • Ignore attendee body language
  • Avoid tough questions
  • Leave without clear next steps

Who is Demo Script Builder not ideal for?

  • Anyone who cannot give the inputs listed above; the answer is built only from them.
  • Teams that want the tools to read their CRM, email or call notes: they work only from what you type.
  • Sending anything straight to a buyer: every output is a draft for a person to review first.

How does Demo Script Builder work?

No sign-in. A web browser is enough, and the answer comes back in a second or two.

  1. Fill in the form above, or press Fill in an example.
  2. Press Run Demo Script Builder.
  3. Read the result on this page, then copy it and edit it before you use it.

It applies fixed rules in the Revenue Enablement code to the inputs above and returns text. It does not call an AI model, does not look anything up, and does not store what you type. The web form and the AI assistant connector run the same tool code, so the same input gives the same answer in both.
