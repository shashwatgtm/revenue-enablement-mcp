Competitive Positioning: vs Competitor A

Situation
  • Competitor: Competitor A
  • Your Solution: Lanehop
  • Evaluation Stage: mid
  • Buyer Persona: Head of Last-Mile Operations

Sector: read from your inputs as logistics tech (from the buyer job titles: your own description names no sector). Business model: software subscription (the usual model in this sector, assumed; set Business model to change it).

Who is evaluating: an operations leader. They care about daily execution, service levels and cost per unit of work, and worry about disruption to live operations and a team that will not use the new way. Ask in those terms.

Competitive Intelligence
Your Strengths
  • Live in six weeks without IT help
  • Cleans unstructured addresses automatically
  • Re-plans routes during the day
Competitor A: Weak Points (your notes, never read out to the buyer)
  • Months of setup before the first route
  • Struggles with unstructured addresses
Sector notes: logistics tech
  • Who usually decides: The COO or Head of Supply Chain signs; the Head of Logistics or Last-Mile Operations champions; fleet and dispatch managers use it daily; IT checks the fit with the TMS, WMS and order systems; finance checks the cost per delivery case.
  • Words this sector's buyers use: dispatch, fleet, last-mile, route plan, first-attempt delivery, proof of delivery, 3PL, cost per delivery, delivery SLA, TMS. Use them where they are true for the prospect.
  • A proof point that lands: A before and after of cost per delivery or first-attempt delivery at one hub, measured over a full cycle of busy and quiet weeks.
Positioning Strategy
Golden Rule

Never go negative. Let the buyer discover Competitor A's weaknesses through their own evaluation.

Discovery Questions (Landmines)

These questions let the buyer find Competitor A's gaps through their own evaluation. The weak points below are your own notes: never read one out to the buyer.

General Competitive Discovery
  • "What other options are you evaluating, and what criteria are you using?"
  • "What's most important to you in making this decision?"
  • "Have you defined must-haves versus nice-to-haves?"
  • "How do you plan routes today, and how often does a dispatcher re-plan during the day?"
  • "What happens when an order, a driver or a road changes after the vehicles have left?"
  • "Which part of the day goes wrong most often, and what does it cost?"
  • "How do you measure it today, and who owns that number?"
Capability Landmines

Their weak point (your note, not for the buyer): Months of setup before the first route

Landmine Question: "How long from signing to the first real result in each option, and what do you need to have ready? Could each show it on your own data?"

Why It Works: The buyer tests the time from signing to the first real result themselves, so the gap shows up in their own evaluation.

Their weak point (your note, not for the buyer): Struggles with unstructured addresses

Landmine Question: "How does each option handle your own data and the systems it must connect to? Could each show it live?"

Why It Works: The buyer tests the handling of your own data and the systems it must connect to themselves, so the gap shows up in their own evaluation.

Implementation Landmines
  • "What is their typical implementation timeline? Have you talked to customers about actual versus promised?"
  • "Who from their team will be involved in implementation?"
  • "What is included in the price and what costs extra?"
Support Landmines
  • "What level of support is included? What happens when you have an urgent issue?"
  • "Can you talk to customers who've been through their support process?"
Stage-Specific Tactics
Mid Stage Recommendations
  • Make sure your differentiators are being tested
  • Provide proof points and references
  • Surface Competitor A's limitations through the buyer's own tests

Use these tactics professionally and honestly: claim nothing you cannot prove.

Who is Competitive Trap Setter not ideal for?

  • Anyone who cannot give the inputs listed above; the answer is built only from them.
  • Teams that want the tools to read their CRM, email or call notes: they work only from what you type.
  • Sending anything straight to a buyer: every output is a draft for a person to review first.

How do I use Competitive Trap Setter from Claude?

Add Revenue Enablement once (see Use it in Claude or ChatGPT), then ask in plain words. A prompt built from the tested example:

Use Revenue Enablement's Competitive Trap Setter tool. competitor: Competitor A; your solution: Lanehop; evaluation stage: Mid; trap type: Discovery questions; buyer persona: Head of Last-Mile Operations; your strengths: live in six weeks without IT help, cleans unstructured addresses automatically and re-plans routes during the day; competitor weaknesses: months of setup before the first route and struggles with unstructured addresses.

How does Competitive Trap Setter work?

No sign-in. A web browser is enough, and the answer comes back in a second or two.

  1. Fill in the form above, or press Fill in an example.
  2. Press Run Competitive Trap Setter.
  3. Read the result on this page, then copy it and edit it before you use it.

It applies fixed rules in the Revenue Enablement code to the inputs above and returns text. It does not call an AI model, does not look anything up, and does not store what you type. The web form and the AI assistant connector run the same tool code, so the same input gives the same answer in both.
