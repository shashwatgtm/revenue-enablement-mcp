MEDDPICC questions
M: Metrics
  • How do you measure first attempt delivery rate today, and who owns that number?
  • If that problem were fixed, which of first attempt delivery rate, failed delivery and return rate or cost per delivery would move first, and by how much would it have to move to matter to you?
  • What does that problem cost each month today in time, money or risk, and how did you arrive at that figure?

  • Can we include the COO or Head of Supply Chain in the next conversation about route planning software for last-mile delivery?
D: Decision criteria
  • What would be a must-have in route planning software for last-mile delivery for you, and what would be nice to have?
  • How much weight do you give first attempt delivery rate when you compare options for route planning software for last-mile delivery?
  • What would make you drop an option for route planning software for last-mile delivery?
D: Decision process
  • What steps does a decision on route planning software for last-mile delivery go through in third-party logistics, and who is involved at each step?
  • What date are you working back from to have that problem fixed, and what happens if it slips?
P: Paper process
  • What do legal, security and procurement need to see before route planning software for last-mile delivery can be signed, and how long does each take?
  • What would those reviewers look at hardest in route planning software for last-mile delivery?
I: Identify pain
  • On "about 1 in 6 deliveries fail on the first attempt": who feels it most, and what does it cost them?
  • On that problem: what happens each week that it stays that way?
C: Champion
  • Besides you, who else wants that problem fixed: your head of IT?
  • If I gave you a business case for route planning software for last-mile delivery, would you take it to the COO or Head of Supply Chain?
  • What would that person need from us to push for route planning software for last-mile delivery?
C: Competition
  • What do you use today to deal with that problem, and what do you like about it?
  • Have you considered building this yourselves, or leaving it as it is?
  • Which other vendors are you speaking to about route planning software for last-mile delivery?
Closing the call
  • Here is what I heard: one problem you want fixed with route planning software for last-mile delivery, with the approval owner still to confirm. Have I got that right?
  • The open points are who signs off spend above $50,000 and what a failed delivery costs today. Who can I speak to about each one?
  • I would suggest we put a baseline on first attempt delivery rate before the next call. Who can give it to us?
If you hear an objection
  • If you hear "we already have a TMS": Ask which daily decisions it does not make today (re planning when orders change, address cleaning, driver allocation) and position the product as working with it, not replacing it.
  • If you hear "drivers will not use a new app": Offer a pilot at one hub with an offline first driver app and agree how adoption is measured before the pilot starts.
  • If you hear "integration effort": Name the systems involved (transport, warehouse, ERP, order management) and the few data fields needed, and agree who on the buyer side owns each.
  • If you hear "our margins are too thin for this": Tie the price to cost per delivery and failed deliveries measured in the buyer's own data, not to a general claim.
Sector notes: logistics tech, last mile delivery
  • Who usually decides: The COO or Head of Supply Chain signs; the Head of Logistics or Last Mile Operations champions; fleet and dispatch managers use it daily; IT checks the fit with the transport, warehouse and order systems; finance checks the cost per delivery case.
  • Words this sector's buyers use: dispatch, fleet, last mile, route plan, first attempt delivery, proof of delivery, third party logistics provider, cost per delivery, delivery SLA, delivery window. Use them where they are true for the prospect.
  • A proof point that lands: A before and after of cost per delivery or first attempt delivery at one hub, measured over a full cycle of busy and quiet weeks.
Reading the answer

Use the question bank to test route planning software for last-mile delivery, beginning with three observations: ask Head of Last-Mile Operations for the most recent instance of "About 1 in 6 deliveries fail on the first attempt"; ask how often it happened and how the cost was counted; then ask what result and whose approval would justify changing the process. The remaining questions are optional probes, not a required script. The conclusion needs review if the buyer cannot confirm a recent instance, the metric counts a different scope or period, or the approval condition does not support a change.

Proposed owners. Confirm the named person before acting.

CEO: have finance verify the scope and period behind "the unverified metric baseline" before valuing the problem; distinguish total team effort from effort per person.

How to read the answer

The bank opens with Unanswered questions first (reminding you to confirm the next missing decision or metric gap before picking from the full list), followed by an Opening for a discovery conversation, role-specific questions (Questions for <Prospect role>), framework questions for every letter of your chosen method (MEDDPICC, BANT, SPICED, Challenger, Gap Selling, or All), and Closing the call. If you leave Known pain points and Known metrics blank, the questions use placeholders like "the problem you came to fix" and "the number this problem moves" so you remember to ask the buyer to name them first.

What to do next

  • Founder: Use the Questions for <Prospect role> and I: Identify pain questions on early founder-led sales calls, and write down the buyer's exact words and baseline numbers.
  • CEO: Check that deals entering the pipeline have dated answers to E: Economic buyer, D: Decision process, and P: Paper process (what legal, security, and procurement need and how long each takes).
  • CMO: Review the answers reps record for M: Metrics and C: Competition across discovery calls to sharpen your website messaging and battle cards.
  • CRO: Require reps to fill in Known pain points, Known metrics, and Gaps to fill before their second call (Deep dive or Technical stage) and record a dated answer to the first unresolved buyer question in the CRM.
  • Demand generation head: Use the top role-specific questions from Questions for <Prospect role> as conversation starters in SDR cold outreach and webinar polls.

Why come back

Come back before every next-stage call (First call, Discovery, Deep dive, Technical, or Executive) and paste the pain points and metrics the buyer just shared so the bank generates targeted follow-up questions on what is still missing. The tool applies the same fixed rules every time. It does not call an AI model, and this site does not keep what you enter.
