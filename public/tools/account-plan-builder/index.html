Strategic account plan: the logistics customer

The account in brief

The logistics customer is an existing account in third-party logistics with annual recurring revenue of $36,000. This plan is about growing it with our product. By this tool's example rule on ARR alone the account sits in the Growth contract band. Expansion potential is unknown until a new need and its buyer are supplied and confirmed; ARR does not establish either.

Industrythird-party logistics
Account TierGrowth (set by this tool's rule from Current ARR)
Current ARR$36,000
Expansion PotentialUnknown; confirm a new need and its buyer before assessing expansion

Sector: not clear from your inputs (name the industry for sector notes). Business model: not stated. Tell us how you charge and the plan will adjust.

Who decides and who influences

Find a champion first: the person who feels the problem and is measured on it. Find out who signs this off and who controls the budget.

Where the account can grow

They use our route planning in 3 of their 12 cities today (in your words). Find out what exactly the account uses of our product today and what it still does by other means; the gap between the two is the whitespace.

Prove the first step before you ask for the next: a before and after on one team, measured on a number the account already tracks.

Alternatives and competition

Find out what the account does today, including doing it by hand: ask who else solves this problem for them, what has worked and not worked, and what would have to change for them to look at another way.

The next 90 days
Days 1 to 30: map the account and listen (by 2026-12-02)
  1. Find the people who own the problem, hold the budget and will use our product every day.
  2. Ask what they use today instead, including doing it by hand.
  3. Agree the baseline: which of the numbers it already tracks the account measures today, and who owns each number.

By day 30 you should have a named champion, a baseline in the account's own numbers, and the missing roles named.

Days 31 to 60: prove the next step (by 2027-01-01)
  1. Agree a proof, with one team and a measure fixed beforehand.
  2. Build the business case in the account's own figures: the ROI Business Case Builder tool needs their cost or value figures, so ask finance for them now.
  3. Put the baseline and the proof result in front of whoever signs.

By day 60 the business case should be accepted by the person who signs, with the proof result in the account's numbers.

Days 61 to 90: decide and widen (by 2027-01-29)
  1. Hold the decision meeting with the person who signs: set the proof result next to the baseline from the first 30 days.
  2. Agree the scope, the term and the price for the step you are asking the account to take.
  3. Check the renewal date and have the expansion agreed before it, so it is not decided inside the renewal.
  4. Agree the next review date and who owns the measure until then.

By day 90 you should have a decision on the expansion.

This week
  • Find and test a champion.
  • Ask who signs, and ask for that person by name.
  • Ask one person who lives with the problem what they do about it today.

To sharpen this plan, give:

  • known contacts (it would give one row for each person, with their part and a next step, in place of the generic roles above)
  • expansion opportunities (it would name the areas to grow into and their sponsors, and fill days 61 to 90)
  • competitive threats (it would give a question and a proof for each alternative the account uses)
  • account notes that start with "Objections:" and list them with semicolons (it would add an answer for each)

The plan is dated from today (2026-11-02); review it monthly.

Who is Account Plan Builder not ideal for?

  • Anyone who cannot give the inputs listed above; the answer is built only from them.
  • Teams that want the tool to read their CRM, email or call notes: it works only from what you type.
  • Anyone who wants to send the output straight to a buyer: every output is a draft for a person to review first.

How do I use Account Plan Builder with an AI assistant?

Add Revenue Enablement once (see Use it in Claude or ChatGPT), then ask in plain words. Here is a prompt built from the tested example:

My role is not stated. Ask me for my role first. Use Revenue Enablement's Account Plan Builder tool. account name: the logistics customer; industry: third-party logistics; current ARR: 36000; your solution: our product; current products: our route planning in 3 of their 12 cities. If this tool is unavailable, ask me to open its web page; do not invent its output. In Gemini CLI or Antigravity, use the Helix GTM Suite skills package for related guidance, without claiming to run this connector.

How does Account Plan Builder work?

There is no sign in, and a web browser is enough. The answer comes back in a second or two.

  1. Fill in the form above, or press Fill in an example.
  2. Press Run Account Plan Builder.
  3. Read the result on this page, then copy it and edit it before you use it.

It applies fixed rules in the Revenue Enablement code to the inputs above and returns text. It does not call an AI model, does not look anything up, and does not store what you type. The web form and the AI assistant connector run the same tool code, so the same input gives the same answer in both.
